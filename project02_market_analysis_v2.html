Section 01 · Business Problem
What We Were Trying to Answer

The product team needed a systematic answer to one question: in a market being disrupted by generative AI, where should we invest next?

The global mobile imaging market in 2025 was undergoing a structural shift. Traditional photo and video editing products — which historically drove the majority of revenue — were showing signs of user growth stagnation. Meanwhile, generative AI was delivering triple-digit growth in both users and monetization. The business needed more than a market overview. It needed a framework for deciding which product directions were genuinely worth investing in — and which were trend noise.

Section 03 · The Core Analytical Contribution
Designing the "Impossible Triangle" Framework
Original Framework · Designed by Analyst

Why one growth dimension is never enough

Standard market analyses tend to rank product categories by a single metric — usually downloads or revenue. The problem: a category can look healthy on one axis while quietly dying on another. High downloads with no monetization is a trend. Strong revenue with declining users is a sunset. High engagement with no new users is a ceiling.

The "Impossible Triangle" framework was designed to solve this by requiring a product direction to demonstrate simultaneous top-performance across all three growth engines before being flagged as a genuine opportunity.

📥

New User Acquisition

Alone: Could be a passing trend. No durability signal.

Download growth indicates that new users are actively discovering and choosing this product direction over alternatives.

🔁

Active Engagement

Alone: Users come but won't pay. Value proposition unclear.

DAU growth indicates that users are returning habitually — the product has embedded itself into a real behavioral routine.

💰

Monetization

Alone: Extracting from an aging base. Growth ceiling approaching.

Revenue growth indicates users are willing to pay — confirming genuine value delivery, not just entertainment or novelty.

△

A direction that ranks in the top 10 across all three dimensions simultaneously signals structural demand — not a trend spike, not extraction from a shrinking base. These are the directions worth betting on.

Section 04 · Technical Execution
From Raw Data to Analysis-Ready

Before any insight could be generated, 690+ products of messy external data needed to be transformed into a structured, comparable dataset.

STEP 01
Data Extraction

Pulled performance data for 690+ products from SensorTower across four metrics: downloads, MAU, DAU, and revenue. Data extracted for both current period and prior year to enable YoY growth calculation across all dimensions.

STEP 02
Cleaning & Normalization

Standardized inconsistent product categorizations, handled missing values for products with limited historical data, and reconciled metric definitions across different product types (subscription vs. ad-supported products report revenue differently in SensorTower).

Key challenge: products with different monetization models required metric normalization before meaningful comparison
STEP 03
Sub-direction Mapping

Mapped each of the 690+ products to one of 48 product sub-directions defined by the product team (based on primary user mental model). Calculated aggregate metrics per sub-direction to enable category-level analysis rather than individual product comparison.

STEP 04
Framework Application

Applied the Impossible Triangle framework across all 48 sub-directions — ranking each on download growth delta, DAU growth delta, and revenue growth delta independently, then identifying which directions achieved simultaneous top-10 ranking across all three.

STEP 05
Portfolio Gap Analysis

Cross-referenced high-opportunity sub-directions against the existing product portfolio to identify where competitive gaps existed — specifically which Impossible Triangle directions had no strong incumbent, or where the existing product had fallen behind market-leading competitors.

Section 06 · What the Analysis Revealed
Three Insights That Shaped the Recommendation

Download growth and monetization growth are structurally decoupled in mobile imaging

The highest-download sub-directions consistently showed weak monetization. The highest-revenue growth sub-directions showed moderate-to-low download growth. This is not a temporary imbalance — it reflects fundamentally different user intent between "try this trend" behavior and "I will pay for this" behavior. Most market analyses pick one dimension and miss this split entirely.

separate acquisition strategy from monetization strategy. Optimizing for downloads in a monetization-weak direction is resource misallocation.

In camera products, competitive moats are built at the asset level, not the app level

Users in the retro camera sub-direction don't have loyalty to apps — they have loyalty to specific filter names and specific film stock emulations. The competitive battleground had shifted from "which app has better UX" to "which app has the filter I want." This means that for any product competing in this space, filter development velocity and asset quality matter more than feature depth.

content strategy IS product strategy in this category. A roadmap focused on app features without a parallel filter content plan will underperform.

AI features show materially higher monetization efficiency than equivalent non-AI features

Across the generative AI sub-directions, revenue growth was consistently outpacing MAU growth — the opposite pattern from most traditional editing categories. Users demonstrated clear willingness to pay a premium for AI-generated outputs that they perceive as beyond their own capability. This monetization efficiency gap between AI and non-AI features is the most significant structural finding of the analysis.

AI features should be positioned as premium conversion drivers, not free engagement hooks. The monetization ceiling for AI features is meaningfully higher than for traditional editing equivalents.
Section 07 · Business Impact
How the Analysis Changed Decisions

The Impossible Triangle framework surfaced AI creative play features as the one sub-direction achieving simultaneous top-performance across all three growth dimensions. This directly informed the product roadmap decision to prioritize AI generative feature development as the primary investment — shifting resources away from incremental iteration on traditional editing capabilities, where the market data showed structural ceilings rather than growth opportunity.
