## The Business Problem

The product team needed a systematic answer to one question: in a market being disrupted by generative AI, which product directions are genuinely worth investing in?

Standard market analyses rank categories by a single metric — usually downloads or revenue. The problem: a direction can look healthy on one axis while quietly dying on another. This project was built to solve that.

---

## The Core Analytical Contribution: The "Impossible Triangle" Framework

The central methodological contribution of this project was designing a three-dimensional growth framework to filter signal from noise.

**The logic:** a product direction only represents a genuine structural opportunity when it demonstrates simultaneous growth across all three engines:

| Dimension | What It Signals | Alone, It Means... |
|---|---|---|
| **Download Growth** | New users are actively choosing this direction | Could be a passing trend — no durability signal |
| **DAU Growth** | Users return habitually — real behavioral embedding | Users come but won't pay — value proposition unclear |
| **Revenue Growth** | Users are willing to pay — genuine value delivery confirmed | Extracting from an aging base — ceiling approaching |

Directions achieving top-10 ranking simultaneously across all three dimensions were classified as "Impossible Triangle" opportunities — structural bets rather than trend plays.

## Methodology

**1. Data Extraction & Pipeline (SensorTower)**
Pulled performance data for 690+ products across downloads, MAU, DAU, and revenue. Built the full data pipeline from extraction through cleaning and normalization — including resolving inconsistent categorizations and reconciling metric definitions across different product monetization models.

**2. Three-Dimensional Growth Analysis**
Applied the Impossible Triangle framework across 48 product sub-directions — ranking each independently on download growth delta, DAU growth delta, and revenue growth delta, then identifying which directions achieved simultaneous top-10 performance.

**3. Portfolio Gap Analysis**
Cross-referenced high-opportunity sub-directions against the existing product portfolio to identify where competitive white-space existed and where the portfolio had fallen behind market-leading competitors.

**4. Cross-Market Validation (7 Regions)**
Extended quantitative findings through collaborative sessions with regional teams across the US, Japan, Korea, Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, and Brazil — surfacing local context that aggregate data couldn't capture.

---

## Key Findings

**1. Download growth and monetization growth are structurally decoupled in mobile imaging.**
The highest-download directions consistently showed weak monetization. Optimizing for downloads in a monetization-weak direction is resource misallocation.

**2. In camera products, competitive moats are built at the asset level, not the app level.**
Users have loyalty to specific filter names — not app names. Filter development velocity and asset quality matter more than feature depth.

**3. AI features show materially higher monetization efficiency than equivalent non-AI features.**
Revenue growth consistently outpaced MAU growth in generative AI directions — the reverse of traditional editing categories. Users demonstrated clear willingness to pay a premium for AI-generated outputs.

---

## Business Impact

The Impossible Triangle framework identified AI creative play features as the one sub-direction achieving simultaneous top-performance across all three growth dimensions. This directly informed the product roadmap decision to prioritize AI generative feature development as the primary investment — shifting resources away from incremental iteration on traditional editing capabilities.
